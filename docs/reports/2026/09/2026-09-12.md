## Top 3 Market Catalysts

### 1. Saudi Arabia shuts East-West pipeline after drone attack, removing a 4–5 million bpd Hormuz bypass

**Status:** Released/Occurred · **Importance:** ★★★★★

Saudi Arabia temporarily shut the 1,200-km East-West pipeline as a precaution after a drone attack. The line had recently moved roughly 4–5 million barrels per day and had become a critical bypass while Strait of Hormuz tanker traffic was constrained. Sep11 reporting described attack damage; by Sep12 Saudi Arabia had confirmed an actual shutdown, converting threatened capacity into a realized routing constraint.

**Transmission:** Pipeline outage → less Hormuz bypass capacity → higher crude/freight premium → higher inflation expectations → higher front-end yields → pressure on duration and cyclicals.

**Confirmation:** Pipeline remains shut or repair timing extends while Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb flows remain impaired; energy futures reopen materially above Friday settlement. **Invalidation:** meaningful throughput is restored quickly and at least one major Gulf/Red Sea shipping route improves without new attacks.

### 2. U.S. August CPI rises 0.4% m/m and pushes September Fed hike odds to roughly 86–90%

**Status:** Released/Occurred / Market Pricing · **Importance:** ★★★★★

August U.S. CPI rose 0.4% month-on-month and 3.4% year-on-year. Core CPI rose 0.3% month-on-month and 2.4% year-on-year; gasoline jumped 3.9%. Rate futures moved to roughly an 86–90% probability of a 25bp Fed hike while the official 2Y Treasury yield closed at 4.63%.

**Transmission:** CPI/core acceleration → higher Fed hike probability → higher 2Y/real yields and tighter dollar liquidity → valuation compression for long-duration and financing-sensitive assets.

**Confirmation:** 2Y yields hold near or above Friday levels and Fed pricing remains strongly tilted to a September hike. **Invalidation:** Fed pricing retreats materially because energy risk reverses or incoming data weakens enough to dominate the inflation impulse.

### 3. Perim seizure and record VLCC freight rates turn Middle East shipping risk into a two-chokepoint cost shock

**Status:** Ongoing / Confirmed · **Importance:** ★★★★

Iran-aligned Houthis reached strategic Perim Island at Bab el-Mandeb while Strait of Hormuz traffic remained sharply reduced. Gulf of Oman-to-China VLCC freight reached roughly Worldscale 450, equivalent to about $11.50 per barrel, as tanker availability and insurance costs tightened.

**Transmission:** Two-chokepoint shipping stress + record freight → higher delivered energy costs → weaker real income and margins → higher inflation expectations → tighter global policy bias.

**Confirmation:** VLCC rates stay near records, Hormuz transits remain depressed and Houthi control around Perim persists through the weekend. **Invalidation:** shipping security improves enough to restore tanker availability and freight rates fall materially.
